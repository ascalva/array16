# array16

[![ci](https://github.com/ascalva/array16/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/ascalva/array16/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

A phased-array software radio designed in code, and a theory-first study of what its fields can do:

> Can a generic process create arbitrary fields that let the array act like an antenna, a virtual antenna? What is
> the theory of such a system, and what are its consequences?

One case of it is a closed guide: a field that holds charged particles on a ring or a knot and accelerates them round
it. The radio is a 4×4 array, a 5.8 GHz board in schematics with a redesign for 0.4-6 GHz, and a HackRF Pro is its
only radio. The work is at its theory, design and simulation stage; boards are designed for fabrication and built once
the designs are understood well enough to finalise (`AGENTS.md` rule 12). The mathematics is proved in Lean,
predictions are committed before the runs that test them, and every tool used or made here is open source.

## What has been found

- **A virtual antenna is a weighted sum of the board's sixteen element fields.** Over a region they span at most
  sixteen dimensions per frequency, and that space's spectrum prices each target before anything is driven. With one
  HackRF, receive weights apply in software after one capture through sixteen switched states, at 1/16 of a static
  beam's signal-to-noise ratio; on transmit, a periodic sequence of states gives weights on comb lines.
- **A charge is held by stored field.** Power radiated in from afar makes a well at most `|q/m|·Z₀·P/(3π·c²)` deep:
  78 µeV per watt for an electron, so 1 eV takes 12.8 kW (RFG-2). Electrodes millimetres from the path, fed through a
  resonator, multiply that by `(3π/2)·(R/Z₀)·share/(kh)²` (RFG-3). Two facing boards keep a quarter of a four-rod
  well, one board at most 0.0091 of it (ELE-2, ELE-3).
- **An RF field alone guides electrons round a knot, and gaps on one chirp accelerate them.** In the first run, four
  rods on a trefoil at 1 GHz held electrons of 0.5 to 8 eV on every lap tracked; four gaps driven by one chirp took a
  50 eV beam to 185 eV in 135 laps and captured 45 % of it.
- **Energy sets a knot's size, and its drive is a compile problem.** A knot turns through more than 4π, so its length
  exceeds 4π times its tightest bend radius. The guide is a ring resonator: against 2 % rms spacer error, two
  re-solved feeds leave a backward wave of 0.013 of the forward, where a fixed hybrid leaves 0.13. The lead design,
  facing flex strips on a trefoil at 915 MHz, takes 0.89 W for a 1 eV well on bare copper, and about 0.33 W with the
  split between each board's halves widened to the gap's half-width; cut into six segments, one chirp of 5.6 to
  15.8 MHz takes electrons from 1 to 8 eV in 117 laps.
- **In a design's solved field, electrons did what the exact theory says** (experiment 2, stage 1: 16 of 19 checks
  pass). Wells lost their first electron where the exact motion predicted; drives past Mathieu's limit held none
  beside a control that held all. Planar rings held their whole static limit, the trefoil and a helix 0.86 and 0.87
  of theirs, so a flat ring of the trefoil's length held 3.2 times as much. Read afterwards, a Hypothesis: leaving the
  plane costs about 14 %, knotted or not.
- **A ring on one board takes bridges and a lower q** (stage 0). In full wave, two rails closed into a ring resonate
  apart (899 and 962 MHz on a backed board), so it takes bridges across its grounded strip or compiled feeds. At q 0.5
  the rails go unstable just under the null, leaving 0.01 to 0.03 eV of the 0.095 eV well, where q 0.3 keeps all of
  it; at 50 V its copper would multipact.
- **A cavity's nulls close into loops only where its symmetry is broken.** In a perfect sphere a real TE field's nulls
  are diameters. Closed null loops take two mode families with different radial profiles at one frequency, which a
  deformed sphere or a tuned cylinder supplies: a copper cylinder tuned that way holds three null loops in a well
  4.8 times the free-space ceiling. A link or a knot takes at least three profiles; none turned up in about 4,300
  random cavity fields or 96 compiled targets, so whether a real cavity field can knot its nulls is open.
- **Capture takes electrons made inside the guide, a guide that changes while they arrive, or cooling:** a guide that
  keeps phase-space volume captures a set of zero volume from outside. On the board, a 265 nm LED on a magnesium pad
  photoemits about 4.3 nA per milliwatt; the electrodes read the beam's number, energy and secular frequency, and the
  beam measures stray fields, the vacuum and, as a wave, rotation and magnetic flux.
- **For a magnetic guide, the twist of the path holds an electron and its knot does not** (experiment 1). The
  first-order theory called held or lost correctly in 92 cases of 93; its laws are proved (GDE-1 to GDE-7).
- **The pieces exist in other fields:** printed ion tracks with turns and levels, electrons guided and trapped on
  microwave boards, RF gaps that accelerate on FR-4. They set numbers for the designs: built board resonators near
  1 kΩ, and copper's lowest multipactor boundary, 27 V near 0.70 GHz·mm.

## Run it

    uv sync --extra dev                  # the environment, from uv.lock
    uv run pytest -q                     # the tests, about two minutes
    uv run ruff check && uv run mypy     # lint and strict types, for the files named in pyproject.toml
    (cd formal && lake exe cache get) && ./formal/check.sh    # the proofs; needs elan, and about 7 GB for Mathlib

Twenty-one tests run the firmware in an emulator, given `node`, rp2040js and a built image (`firmware/README.md`).
Each study script writes its figures, `run.log` and `results.json` into a folder beside it (`--quick` for a fast
pass):

    uv run python experiments/virtual_antenna_study.py   # about 25 s
    uv run python experiments/knotted_guide_study.py     # about 5 min
    uv run python experiments/cavity_knots_study.py      # about 25 s
    uv run python experiments/beam_lifecycle_study.py    # a few seconds

An experiment on the harness runs in three steps, the second once the first is committed:

    uv run python experiments/guide_study.py predict     # the theory's predictions, for every shot
    uv run python experiments/guide_study.py run         # the measurements not yet on file; --bench NAME
    uv run python experiments/guide_study.py report      # the verdicts, by criteria fixed beforehand

Experiment 2's calibration rung takes the same steps on the twin bench, its tracking about two hours:

    uv run python experiments/twin_rung1.py predict                # theory only, from the solved sections
    uv run python experiments/twin_rung1.py run --bench twin_r1    # the tracking; resumable, --only for one shot
    uv run python experiments/twin_rung1.py report                 # the verdicts
    uv run python experiments/twin_rung1_figures.py                # the report's figures, from the records

A first session with the simulated board:

    from array16 import Array
    from array16.sim import SimBackend, Source

    sim = SimBackend("hackrf")                # the HackRF alone
    arr = Array(sim)
    sim.sources = [Source(0, 0, -75)]         # a test source straight ahead
    arr.calibrate()
    sim.sources = [Source(25, 30, -75)]       # a steady carrier: one channel finds it by snapshots
    print(arr.locate())                       # about (25, 30) degrees

## How the work is done

Theory first, its mathematics proved in Lean over a model of the code. The instrument is calibrated against closed
forms before an experiment uses it. Predictions and what would count as their failure are committed before the run
(`array16.lab` enforces it), and what is read from results afterwards is kept apart as a hypothesis. A case that must
fail stands beside every test, and every claim is labelled Proposition, Claim or Hypothesis in `docs/LAWS.md`. An
experiment runs on any bench, a simulator or the hardware it stands in for. New Python is typed (`mypy --strict`),
linted (`ruff`), tested (pytest, Hypothesis) and run through `uv`.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `array16/` | The radio's host library and simulator, `board.py` (the one description of the hardware), the particle core (`particles/`) and the harness (`lab.py`) |
| `array16/guide/` | The guide's instruments: board cross-sections solved by finite volumes, the ring resonator, multipactor |
| `array16/twin/` | The twin bench: a design's guide, detector and vacuum as simulated devices of a bench |
| `designs/` | Design files for the twin bench: band, cross-section, path and frame, drive, tolerances |
| `benches/` | Resource setups: compute, and devices by role |
| `experiments/` | Studies and experiments, each a script, its outputs and a report |
| `docs/` | The theory (`VIRTUAL_ANTENNA`, `KNOTTED_GUIDE`, `CAVITY_KNOTS`, `BEAM_LIFECYCLE`), `PRIOR_ART`, the ledger (`LAWS`), the state and plan (`PROGRESS`) |
| `formal/` | The Lean proofs behind the ledger, and their gate, `check.sh` |
| `firmware/` | The RP2040 beam controller: a portable core, and PIO, DMA, clocks and USB around it |
| `hw/` | The circuit as a script (`design.py`) and the KiCad project it generates |
| `tests/` | The code against closed forms and its models, the studies' numbers, the ledger against the code |
| `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `REDESIGN.md` | The radio: how its pieces fit, and the 0.4-6 GHz proposal |
| `AGENTS.md` | Orientation for agents, and the owner's rules |
