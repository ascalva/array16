# Progress

Where the work stands, what was decided, and what comes next, written so that a fresh reader or agent can pick
it up. `AGENTS.md` holds the owner's rules; this file holds the state. Updated 2026-11-22.

## 1. The question

The owner, on 2026-11-20:

> the point of all this theory and experiments is can you build a generic process for creating arbitrary fields that
> can allow this to act like an antenna, a virtual antenna? studying that and understanding the theory aspect and
> consequences of such as system

That is the aim, and `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` holds its theory. Every field the array makes at one frequency is a
weighted sum of its sixteen embedded element fields, `e = Gw` over a sampled region, so one loop makes any antenna
whose field over that region lies in the span of G act as a virtual antenna: measure G, read its spectrum, pose a
target, solve in the metric of radiated power, map the weights onto the board's states, check on held-out positions,
update. The spectrum states beforehand what each target costs.

The particle question is one case of that synthesis. Can the field generated by the array form a closed geometric
shape that holds charged particles and accelerates them along the path? What do different geometries and
configurations yield, and what do the fields produced by the particles themselves add? A closed guide is a
synthesised field, a null line with a well round it driven as a travelling wave, and as a radiator it is an antenna
with a dial (`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` section 7.2). The owner's earlier questions, whether the accelerator's geometry
could serve as a generic antenna, whether antennas pointed inward make containment fields, and what complex, exotic
fields are good for, are answered there in sections 7.2, 7.3 and 8.1. The work is theory first, then prediction, then
test; ideas may stay theoretical but must be physically and experimentally plausible.

## 3. What has been found

**What a watt buys in a well** (ledger section VI, `array16/particles/rf.py`).
- Pseudo-potential depth `e²E²/(4mω²)`. For an electron, 1 eV takes 12 kV/m at 0.4 GHz, 73 kV/m at 2.45 GHz,
  174 kV/m at 5.8 GHz; thermal energy (25 meV) takes 1.9, 11.6 and 27 kV/m (`experiments/accelerator/run.log`).
- RFG-2 (Proposition, on a cited theorem): power carried in from far away by a field without sources near a point
  makes its strongest field there at the centre of a converging electric dipole wave, so its deepest well is
  `|q/m|·Z₀·P/(3π·c²)` at every frequency: 78.2 µeV per watt for an electron, and 1 eV takes 12.8 kW. Spread over a
  spot `(λ/2)²` wide, the same power gives a field squared 2π/3 times weaker. That no such field carrying P is stronger
  at the point is Bassett's theorem, cited; Lean proves the algebra that rests on it. A structure that returns the
  wave passes the ceiling by its stored energy: a surround dense enough to close, or an element's own near field
  (`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` section 4.5).
- RFG-3 (Proposition): the same power fed through a resonator of shunt resistance R puts `V = √(2PR)` on electrodes
  a distance h from the null and gains `(3π/2)·(R/Z₀)·share/(kh)²` over the ceiling: the resonator stores what free
  space would radiate (R/Z₀), and the electrodes gather the field onto h instead of a wavelength (1/(kh)²). At volts V
  the well is `share·V·q/(8γ)`, q the null's Mathieu q and γ its gradient over V/h², so the slope holds while q stays
  under 0.908 and a depth is stated with its q (`rf.electrode_drive`, `rf.electrode_drive_at_q`). At 1 GHz,
  h = 0.5 mm and 10 kΩ that is 1.14 × 10⁶ and 89.1 eV per watt on four rods up to 2.9 eV at 32 mW, 2.85 × 10⁵ and
  22.3 eV per watt on two facing boards up to 1.2 eV at 53 mW.
- RFG-4 (Proposition): at a guide's space-charge limit the plasma frequency is √2 times the secular frequency,
  `f_p = q·f/2`, whatever the particle: 300 MHz for electrons at q = 0.6 in a 1 GHz guide, at 1.12 × 10¹⁵ per cubic
  metre.
- Shunt resistance, `R = Q/(ωC)` for a resonator whose electrodes hold capacitance C (estimates, which RFG-3 turns
  into depth). A metal cavity of 0.25 to 1 MΩ turns 3.2 mW into 40 to 80 V and 1 W into 0.7 to 1.4 kV. A resonator
  printed on the board, Q of 50 to 200 with sub-picofarad electrodes, is about 10 to 100 kΩ: 8 to 25 V from 3.2 mW and
  140 to 450 V from 1 W. Long electrodes hold more capacitance: the 277 mm guide laid as a ring with 50 pF per metre
  and Q 150 is 1.6 kΩ and holds 13 V with 53 mW (`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` section 7.2), and the nearest measured
  board resonator, the half-wave coplanar line of Matthiesen et al.'s electron trap at 1.6 GHz, reached about 1 kΩ at
  a Q near 35. `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` budgets from 1 to 10 kΩ until a pickup measures the board's own volts
  against watts.
- Electron mean free path (cross-section 1e-19 m², an estimate): 0.4 m at 1e-3 mbar, 40 m at 1e-5 mbar.

**Electrodes printed on boards** (ledger section VII, `array16/particles/electrodes.py`; the gapless model, gaps small
against the distance to the null).
- ELE-1 (Proposition): two rails at V either side of a grounded strip, from `a/2` to `a/2 + b`, put the field's null
  at `h = ½√(a(a + 2b))` above the strip, the geometric mean of the rails' edge distances, with gradient
  `4Vb/(πh(a + b)²)` there. Lean proves where the null stands and the gradient there, as the derivative of the
  complex field.
- ELE-3 (Claim): one board's well leaks through a saddle on the axis at `h·√(1 + w)`, `w = (a + b)/h`, and keeps at
  most `(5√5 − 11)/(2π²)` = 0.0091 of a four-rod well's depth at the same volts and distance, at rail/gap 3.68 with
  the saddle 2.06 null heights up.
- ELE-2 (Claim): two boards facing across 2h, each split into halves at ±V/2, have potential
  `−(V/π)·atan(tan(πy/2h)·tanh(πx/2h))`, a null at the centre and a barrier `V/2h`: a quarter of a four-rod well, at
  least 27 times one board's.
- 0.5 mm from the null, through 10 kΩ and at q = 0.5, 1 eV takes 16 V and 13 mW on four rods at 1.07 GHz, 50 V and
  126 mW on two facing boards at 1.68 GHz, and 542 V and 14.7 W on one board at 3.46 GHz. At 1 GHz four rods take 15 V
  and 11 mW at q = 0.53, while one board's null would sit at q = 1.73, past Mathieu's 0.908 (RFG-3).

**The first run** (`experiments/accelerator_study.py`, on the core since a4b2d71 and reported in
`experiments/accelerator_study.md`; point charges, thin rods; it reproduces the run of 2026-11-16, and its magnetic
guide is now experiment 1's).
- 1 GHz quadrupole of four rods 0.5 mm from a trefoil axis (length 277 mm, tightest bend 16.6 mm). At Mathieu
  q = 0.3: 13 V rod to rod, secular 106 MHz, depth 0.32 eV, held 0.5-2 eV, lost 4 and 8 eV. At q = 0.6: 25 V,
  212 MHz, 1.26 eV, held 0.5-8 eV, lost 16 and 32 eV. Centrifugal estimate of the largest energy held:
  depth × bend radius / (2 r₀) = 5.2 and 20.9 eV, about twice what tracking held.
- 60 Hz, ±500 V, r₀ 6 mm trefoil (814 mm) in air: 10 µm glass grains held at half, one and two times the nominal
  charge; a 7 kV/m travelling wave (34 V on a 30 mm period) carries them round at 5 cm/s, a lap in 16.6 s.
  Grains closer than 0.65 mm along the guide buckle from a line into a zigzag.
- Four 0.5 V gaps on a 932 mm loop, harmonic 13, one chirp 58.5 → 112.6 MHz in 21 µs with 90° between gaps: a
  continuous 50 eV beam reaches 185 eV in 135 laps, 45 % captured, 1.6 eV rms spread. On a 50 Ω pickup coupling
  30 %, 1e5 to 1e7 circulating electrons give about -98 to -58 dBm (an estimate).
- Cyclotron resonance at 2.45 GHz needs 87.5 mT uniform to 1e-4: 17 V/m gives 48 eV in 10 µs, 10 kV/m gives
  3.4 keV. It needs a static field, so it sits outside the RF-first plan, as the optional section `resonance`.
- Read against the board (the review of 2026-11-20; the report's sections 2, 7 and 8 now say so):
  - The 1 GHz guide holds one RF voltage along its 277 mm, 0.92 of a wavelength at 1 GHz. A single-fed electrode that
    long carries a standing wave, with a node every half guided wavelength and no well at the nodes. A closed guide
    that long runs as a travelling-wave ring (section 4, N3); a guide fed at one point is kept short against λ/4.
  - The electron cases use free-standing rods. On one board the same volts keep at most 0.0091 of the depth, about
    1 % (ELE-3); two facing boards keep a quarter (ELE-2).
  - The first run's 0.25 to 1 MΩ suits a metal cavity. A board resonator is about 10 to 100 kΩ (above): 3.2 mW gives
    about 8 to 25 V and 1 W about 140 to 450 V.

**Experiment 1** (`experiments/guide_study.md`; registered at 6c058df, run once, recorded at 85a3ff6). A
magnetic guide wound on five closed curves, 142 shots, ten criteria fixed beforehand.
- A flat ring lost all 10 electrons within 0.03 lap of the predicted lap. A trefoil, a (1,3) helix on a torus
  and a lifted figure-eight all held electrons. The unknotted helix held four times the gyroradius the trefoil
  did on axis.
- First-order theory: held or lost right in 92 of 93, reach within 15 % in 54 of 55, closed orbit in 21 of 22;
  turn per lap off by more than 8° in 7 fits of 22 (up to 16.5°), so the registered test failed on that.
- Read afterwards (hypothesis GDE-H2): the shift shared by both directions is ρ × bending / 2, to 2 % on seven
  of eight pairs. The reversing part matches how far the coil's own field lines turn beyond the holonomy on the
  helix (5.8°) and figure-eight (0.3°); on the trefoils that check was not clean and their 6.7° and 1.1° are
  open. The closed orbit's second-order error is open.
- Stated in its protocol: one shot was tracked for two laps in a timing test before registration.

**The virtual antenna** (`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md`, its labels kept; model numbers from Hertzian current elements).
- **One process, and the size of "arbitrary".** The fields reachable over a region are range(G), at most sixteen
  dimensions per frequency (Proposition), with a price list `1/σᵢ²` from `GᴴGv = σ²(R + rI)v` known before anything
  is driven: about min(N, the source's count, 2L(L + 2)) modes are cheap (Claim SYN-11), and fine structure costs
  exponentially more (Claim SYN-12). Inside the HackRF's 20 MHz every comb line carries the one waveform with a
  pattern of its own (Claim SYN-14, from the space-time form, Proposition TMA-12), so a field whose time course
  differs from place to place takes more band, across true-time delays or a scattering room.
- **What a virtual antenna carries:** pattern, polarisation, apparent position and receive response over its region;
  port, body, noise and power stay with the board. In empty space a field-only virtual antenna is a focus (Claim).
- **One radio.** With the HackRF Pro alone any receive weight applies in software after one capture through N
  switched states, down to the calibration floor (−24 dB at 3° and 0.26 dB), at 1/N of the static beam's
  signal-to-noise ratio, 12 dB for 16 (Proposition); by hypothesis 8-bit samples bound a software null near 50 dB,
  so nulls of strong signals belong in the board's states. On transmit a periodic state sequence makes comb lines
  with continuous weights (line-1 ceiling 0.900 on rev A, 0.980 to 0.992 on rev B) for signals narrower than the comb
  spacing, 5.88 kHz on rev A and 3.92 kHz on rev B; transmit numbers stay predictions until V1's transmit sessions
  measure the transmit operator.
- **The guide is an antenna with a dial.** N perturbations on a ring carrying mode m radiate only orbital orders
  l = m − pN (Proposition); switched stubs move its radiation Q from about 3 × 10⁸ to 20 (Hypothesis); a guide that
  radiates a fraction η of its drive keeps 1 − η of its well (Proposition).
- **Antennas pointed inward** gather at most RFG-2's ceiling from fields carried in from far away, and in the model
  stored field passes it (Claim): a surround dense enough to close, at its cavity resonances (256 elements at
  kb = 6.1, 939 times lossless and 12 times with 10 % loss), or a focus in an element's own near field (100 mm above
  the whips at 433 MHz, 4.9 times RFG-2's ceiling, 8.9 times the ceiling over the ground plane). Sixteen elements on a
  shell stay within 1.57 of it (1.04 with 10 % loss) at kb ≥ 3. Foci at the 15.249 level give 0.002 to 0.024 µeV, so
  containment runs through the board's own electrodes behind a resonator (RFG-3), at a stated Mathieu q.
- **Charges as antennas.** A held electron column is a series L-C on its electrodes, 142 H per electron across 2 mm,
  heard there 51 dB above its radiation (Claims).
- **V1, predicted** (four switched monopoles at 2.44 GHz fitted to a fifth, scored on held-out positions): −31.9 dB at
  the centre of the square, −15.0 dB a quarter wavelength across the region, −30.7 dB a quarter wavelength away from
  it, −2.4 dB a wavelength across (the case that must fail); generalised spectrum 0, −5.9, −22.3 and −28.6 dB.

**The knotted guide** (`docs/KNOTTED_GUIDE.md`, its labels kept).
- **Levels and pieces.** One rigid two-layer board makes a planar ring. On parallel levels joined by ramps a knot of
  bridge number b needs at least 2b ramps, four for the trefoil; a slot along a closed path frees an island, c + 2
  pieces for a diagram of c crossings and two for the ring.
- **Size.** A knot turns through more than 4π, so its length exceeds 4π times its tightest bend radius (Proposition
  KNT-3): by the bend rule at h = 0.5 mm, at least 12.6 mm of path per unit of held energy over well depth, and 16 to
  33 mm by what tracking held.
- **Drive.** The knot is a ring resonator of shunt resistance Z₀Q/(2πm). Against 2 % rms spacer error two re-solved
  feeds leave a backward wave of 0.013 of the forward in the median and keep 0.94 of a perfect ring's depth per watt,
  where a fixed hybrid leaves 0.13 and 0.74 (60 draws); one feed makes a standing wave.
- **The lead design, T915-S:** facing flex strips on the r/R = 0.45 trefoil at 915 MHz, m = 1, 318 mm on 25 µm
  polyimide (n_eff 1.031). A 1 eV well at q = 0.5 takes 50.3 V: 0.89 W on bare copper (Q 214), 1.1 to 4.2 W on the
  fab's ENIG. Cut into six DC-isolated segments (T915-S6), one chirp of 5.60 to 15.84 MHz takes electrons from 1 to
  8 eV in 117 laps, at about 1.1 × 10⁻⁵ mbar.
- **The split as a lever** (Claim). The copper's loss sits at the edges of the split between each board's halves. At
  the same 1 eV well, widening the split from 0.3h to h raises Q from 210 to 290, closes the gap to 0.72 mm to keep q,
  and cuts the power from 0.88 to 0.33 W (0.26 W at 1.25h); at 1.5h the well leaks through the split.
- **Particles.** Facing boards bent in their plane hold 0.605·D·R/h by the static balance; joints of the null-step
  family heat unless smooth over three secular wavelengths, 29 mm at 8 eV. A bunch on the trefoil is an axial dipole at
  3f_rev and an in-plane one at f_rev and 2f_rev, where on a ring it is an in-plane dipole at f_rev alone: the beam's
  harmonics carry the knot's symmetry.
- **Building.** The twist that closes the strips makes them non-developable, and their two plates differ by 23 mm;
  the stacked boards' slot guide holds 85 % of its field in the laminate (Q 43 on FR-4, 117 on RO4003C, on 35 µm
  copper); copper multipacts above wells of 2 to 3 eV
  (Hypothesis). The flat facing-board ring of the same length holds 2.7 to 3.3 times T915-S: the price of the knot.
- **For the compiler:** topology and geometry become its step 0, and one copper carries three operators at once (DC
  steering, the megahertz chirp, the 915 MHz ring), each with its own price list. The ring's G has two cheap modes, ±m,
  over a floor about 125 times lower.

**Cavity knots** (`docs/CAVITY_KNOTS.md`, its labels kept).
- **A perfect sphere gives rays.** A real standing TE field is `E = ∇ψ × r` in a sphere and `∇Π × ẑ` in a cylinder, so
  it vanishes at the critical points of ψ on concentric spheres (of Π on horizontal slices): curves, generically.
  Within one degenerate family the nulls are diameters and zero-field shells, and a perfect sphere's families never
  share a frequency (Propositions).
- **Loops, links and knots.** Two radial (axial) profiles at one frequency give closed null loops, all unlinked; a
  link or a knot takes at least three (Proposition). None turned up in about 4,300 random cavity fields or 96
  compiled targets: whether a real TE cavity field can hold one is open (Hypothesis), and a threading rule asks about
  half a turn of the field's principal axes along each loop (Claim).
- **Folds.** A null curve of knot or link type K has at least 2b(K) folds against height, 4 for a trefoil
  (Proposition). The wall softens there only along the curve; the leaks are the lowest saddle of |E|² and
  near-reconnections.
- **A worked cylinder:** copper, 13.8 cm in radius and 19.0 cm tall, TE113 and TE221 with real weights from two feeds
  driven as sum and difference, Q 81,658. Three closed null loops 32 to 34 cm long, at least 1.9 cm from every wall,
  sit in a well of 375 µeV per watt, 4.8 times RFG-2's ceiling; board electrodes behind a resonator reach 22 to
  89 eV per watt (RFG-3). 1 eV takes 2.66 kW at 1.49 MV/m peak. The two families stay within half a linewidth for
  about 1 µm on the radius, or with a tuning screw or pre-compensation in the compile.

**The beam's lifecycle** (`docs/BEAM_LIFECYCLE.md`, its labels kept).
- **Capture.** A closed guide that keeps phase-space volume captures only a set of zero volume from outside
  (Proposition): capture takes electrons made inside, a guide that changes while they arrive, or cooling. A 265 nm LED
  on a magnesium pad photoemits 4.3 nA per milliwatt; 10⁷ electrons in one 1 eV turn of the 932 mm ring take 1.0 µA,
  0.24 W of UV or a 2000 K filament.
- **Acceleration.** A bunch oscillates about the synchronous phase at 0.852 MHz at every energy, the drive chirps
  linearly at 2.63 MHz per µs, the spread grows as W^½, bends ask a deeper guide (4.2 eV for 185 eV round 43.8 mm),
  gas heats held electrons, and joints act as stopbands.
- **Their fields:** 62 V/m and 5.5 nT at 0.5 mm from 10⁷ electrons; a resonance shift N⟨U⟩/W, 89 kHz for 1.5 × 10⁷
  electrons; 1.1 × 10⁻²⁴ W of radiation each; Smith-Purcell emission at v/d. β is 0.0020 at 1 eV and 0.027 at
  185 eV; speed shows first as the drive's Doppler shift, raising an electron's q by 3 to 6 % at 185 eV.
- **Out, sensed, and what it measures:** a gate, a junction or the guide's volts send electrons to a Faraday pad, a
  retarding barrier or an MCP; left in place, the electrodes read number, energy, spread and secular frequency. The
  beam measures stray fields, surface field noise, the vacuum and cross-sections, and as a wave the Earth's rotation
  (0.057 rad round 932 mm) and magnetic flux (1 rad per 9.5 fT).

**What the prior art sets** (`docs/PRIOR_ART.md`).
- Built board resonators reach Q 35 to 41 and about 1 kΩ; 100 kΩ asks Q near 3,000 on a 50 Ω half-wave line. Depth
  per watt on built boards sits about ten times below the 10 kΩ examples above.
- Copper's lowest two-surface multipactor boundary is 27.4 V near 0.70 GHz·mm: the first run's 25 V rods sit 0.8 dB
  under it, facing boards reach it at wells of 2.5 to 3 eV, and the tracker takes a secondary-emission model.
- The drive does the heating: a board trap lost its electrons to the drive's 6th and 7th subharmonics, so f_sec stays
  clear of Ω/n for small n, and nothing in the HackRF's drive, aliases included, sits at f_sec, 2f_sec or Ω ± f_sec.
- The 932 mm ring's 126 m acceleration keeps 73 % of its electrons unscattered at 10⁻⁶ mbar, 4.5 % at 10⁻⁵.
- An RF null cannot pass straight through a crossing (Wesenberg 2009): joints are shaped from solved fields.
- SLIM-Pickins, an open-source KiCad layout from a path string, is a starting point for generating the boards.

## 4. Theory leads

Leads to derive properly, prove where they are mathematics, and test, each marked with its status. N3 to N7 keep the
names of the review of 2026-11-20, whose N1 (the ceiling and the electrode gain) and N2 (one board or two) are now
ledger rows RFG-2 to RFG-4 and ELE-1 to ELE-3; the scaling law and the rail null of the earlier list are RFG-1 and
ELE-1. The theory of the virtual antenna itself, with the laws it proposes and the ones to prove first, is
`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md`.

1. **N3. A closed guide is a ring resonator: the travelling-wave ring** (derived; in `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md`
   sections 7.2 and 7.3, where the ring selection rule, Bragg splitting and radiate-or-hold are Propositions proposed
   as NFD-5 to NFD-7 and the stub dial is Hypothesis NFD-H1). A ring whose circumference is m guided wavelengths
   carries cos mφ and sin mφ at one frequency; driven 90° apart at feeds a quarter guided wavelength apart they sum
   to `e^(i(mφ − ωt))`, one amplitude all the way round, with the resonator's gain. One channel through a 90° hybrid
   makes both drives. The 277 mm guide as a ring (R = 44.1 mm) has m = 1 at 1.08 GHz in air (n_eff = 1; on laminate it
   falls as 1/n_eff), and electrons of a few eV, near
   10⁶ m/s, are far slower than the wave, so they see a steady well of one depth all round. Next: the ring's modes,
   Q and R/Q from an eigenmode run (openEMS, or Palace, which refines its mesh by itself), then the ledger.
2. **N4. What a ring hears of its own beam: Shockley-Ramo pickups and feedback** (derived; in
   `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` section 7.4 as Claims CHG-4 and CHG-5 and Hypothesis CHG-H2). An electrode that makes a
   field `E_w` per volt hears a charge q moving at v as a current `q v·E_w`, so a guide's electrodes are its pickups;
   charge rotating round the ring makes fields whose azimuthal order is locked to their frequency, and pickups round
   it read them as a circular array reads orbital orders. Reading the beam to correct the drive is the principle of
   stochastic cooling, and the route by which the particles' own fields make the shape more exact. Next: CHG-1 to
   CHG-3 on the ledger and in Lean, and a pickup observer for `integrate` that sums `q v·E_w` with the field of
   `RailPair` or `FacingBoards` at one volt.
3. **N5. Knotted nulls and reconnection walls** (Hypothesis SYN-H4, `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md` section 4.7). Nulls of
   one complex field component are lines, and topologically protected: a knotted null unties only at a reconnection,
   so the drive space CP¹⁵ splits into regions of fixed knot type separated by walls of real codimension one, and a
   drive's distance to the nearest wall is its knot's margin. Milnor polynomials `uᵖ − v^q` give knotted nulls in
   closed form (the trefoil is p = 2, q = 3). Next: fit a Milnor field's linear conditions on a curve, trace the
   drive's nulls with the tracer of `experiments/local_fields.py` and read the type; the walls are a discriminant in
   the drive's coefficients, a statement for algebraic geometry. These are zero lines of one complex component;
   whole-vector nulls of a real standing field are another object, and the cavity study (`docs/CAVITY_KNOTS.md`)
   finds they need three profiles at one frequency for a link or a knot, with none found yet.
4. **N6. An RF guide needs no twist** (argued; its cost rests on CUR-3). A pseudo-potential pulls back from every
   side, so an RF guide holds a particle round a flat ring; the twist rule of experiment 1 belongs to magnetic
   guides. A knot only costs an RF guide: its largest curvature exceeds 4π/L against a ring's 2π/L (CUR-3), so by the
   bend rule (item 6) it holds less than half the energy of a ring of the same length and electrodes. Knots earn
   their place in path length per volume. Next: a flat ring of the first run's rods tracked on the core beside the
   trefoil, and the statement on the ledger.
5. **N7. Mathieu similarity** (holds; RFG-1 is stated whatever the particle). Mathieu's equation depends on q and the
   damping, not on the particle, so a charged microparticle at 60 Hz to a few kHz runs the electron guide's
   equations: in air a 10 µm grain is overdamped (a Stokes rate of 1450 per second), and near 1 Pa it circulates as
   an accelerator's particle does. The first run's 60 Hz dust guide and its calibration against the damped
   pseudo-potential use it. The owner is not sure a microparticle ring driven at kHz counts as RF first ("I'm not
   sure it does", section 6); it stays a check on the theory and not the next build until the owner decides.
6. **Energy held round a bend** (`rf.bend_limit_ev`; an estimate, checked within a factor two by the first run). An
   RF guide holds kinetic energy up to about `W = D/(2κ_max r₀)`, so `W ≈ (q/16)·eV·(R/r₀)` for bend radius R. Energy
   held grows with volts times R/r₀, with no ceiling other than the voltage: at q = 0.3 and r₀ = 1 mm, 100 eV takes
   53 V at 1.25 GHz with R/r₀ = 100, and 1 keV takes 530 V near 4 GHz. This is the statement to prove in answer to
   "are RF fields enough at any energy".
7. **The field of an electrode pattern from its outline** (a lead to verify before use). For an electrode at V in a
   grounded plane the potential is V/2π times the solid angle it subtends, so its field is a line integral round its
   outline of the Biot-Savart form, `E = (V/2π)∮ dl × (x − x′)/|x − x′|³` (Oliveira and Miranda). If a calibration
   against `RailPair` confirms it, `Coil.field`'s kernel computes the RF field of any PCB pattern from its outline.
8. **Mathematics to use.** Complex analysis, in use: ELE-1 and ELE-2 are a zero and a conformal map of analytic
   fields, and N rails put their nulls at the roots of a polynomial, a root count Mathlib has. Geometry of curves, in
   use: writhe, holonomy, Fenchel, Fáry-Milnor. Algebraic geometry: Milnor polynomials and the walls of N5.

### Backlog

Ideas worth investigating later, kept with what is known of them; none is scheduled.

- **The slice stack** (owner, 2026-11-22): a stack of many boards, each a slice of the three-dimensional guide,
  carrying the guide's cross-section where the path crosses it. Two forms. Stacked rings, each board a plate with a
  hole and neighbours at opposite RF phase, as in stacked-ring ion guides and ion funnels (built on PCBs for ions):
  a flat-bottomed well with steep walls and no null line, its ripple along the axis about 1/I₀(πr₀/d)², 20 % of the
  wall for 1 mm holes on 1.6 mm boards, 0.9 % at 2 mm and 0.03 % at 3 mm. Or a quadrupole in every slice: four plated
  holes per board round the path, aligned through the stack, make four rods of board copper. That brings the first
  run's geometry under rule 1: thin rods kept 0.67 of an ideal four-rod well where facing boards keep 0.25, so a
  well takes about 2.7 times fewer volts. The price is geometry. A closed path turns round in height at least 2b
  times, four for the trefoil, the same count as the ramps of section 3 of the knotted guide and the folds of a
  cavity's knotted null. Sliced along the best of 4,000 directions, T915-S's trefoil crosses the boards at 45° or
  more for 43 % of its length and runs within 30° of them for 44 %, through 36 boards of 1.6 mm (57 mm), so the
  stack is a hybrid: holes where the path is steep, a facing pair in the gap between two boards where it is
  shallow, and compiled ramps between them. To weigh: every board interface is a joint of a periodic structure
  (stopbands at the energies its harmonics meet), each rod's continuity across about 36 interfaces (or each slice
  fed on its own, which makes the stack a phased array along the path for the compiler), alignment through the
  stack, and the laminate's outgassing.
