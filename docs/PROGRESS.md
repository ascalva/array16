# Progress

Where the work stands, what was decided, and what comes next, written so that a fresh reader or agent can pick
it up. `AGENTS.md` holds the owner's rules; this file holds the state. Updated 2026-11-18.

## 1. The question

Can the field generated by the array form a closed geometric shape that holds charged particles and accelerates
them along the path? What do different geometries and configurations yield, and what do the fields produced by
the particles themselves add? The work is theory first, then prediction, then test; ideas may stay theoretical
but must be physically and experimentally plausible.

**Harness** (`array16/lab.py`). An `Experiment` supplies shots, `predict`, `measure`, `judge`; the harness gives
it `predict`, `run`, `report`. `run` starts once the predictions and the experiment's `registered` files are
committed, measures what is not on file in a process pool, and stamps each record with commit, predictions hash,
bench and versions. `report` judges and states the record's integrity. A bench (`benches/*.toml`) names compute
and devices by role; a device's driver is any callable `module:attribute`. `benches/simulated.toml` offers the
simulated board as the `array`.

**Laws and proofs** (`docs/LAWS.md`, `formal/`). Eighteen rows, checked against code, tests and Lean by
`tests/test_laws.py`.
- GDE-1 to GDE-7 (Propositions, `formal/Array16/Guide.lean`): the lap map `z ↦ exp(iα)(z + ρG + H)` has one
  closed orbit when the plane turns, orbits stay on circles about it, a flat curve drifts without bound, and
  reach is convex in ρ.
- CUR-2 (Proposition, `formal/Array16/Curve.lean`): bending ≥ total curvature² / length.
- CUR-1, CUR-3, INT-1 to INT-3, COI-1 to COI-3 (Claims): holonomy is writhe mod 1; Fenchel and Fáry-Milnor on

## 3. What has been found

**Scales for radio-frequency confinement** (closed forms, `experiments/accelerator/run.log`).
- Pseudo-potential depth `e²E²/(4mω²)`. For an electron, 1 eV takes 12 kV/m at 0.4 GHz, 73 kV/m at 2.45 GHz,
  174 kV/m at 5.8 GHz; thermal energy (25 meV) takes 1.9, 11.6 and 27 kV/m.
- A resonant gap of shunt resistance 0.25-1 MΩ (an estimate) turns 3.2 mW into 40-80 V and 1 W into 0.7-1.4 kV.
- Electron mean free path (cross-section 1e-19 m², an estimate): 0.4 m at 1e-3 mbar, 40 m at 1e-5 mbar.

**The first run** (`experiments/accelerator_study.py`, 2026-11-16; point charges, thin rods).
- 1 GHz quadrupole of four rods 0.5 mm from a trefoil axis (length 277 mm, tightest bend 16.6 mm). At Mathieu
  q = 0.3: 13 V rod to rod, secular 106 MHz, depth 0.32 eV, held 0.5-2 eV, lost 4 and 8 eV. At q = 0.6: 25 V,
  212 MHz, 1.26 eV, held 0.5-8 eV, lost 16 and 32 eV. Centrifugal estimate of the largest energy held:
  depth × bend radius / (2 r₀) = 5.2 and 20.9 eV, about twice what tracking held.
- 60 Hz, ±500 V, r₀ 6 mm trefoil (814 mm) in air: 10 µm glass grains held at half, one and two times the nominal
  charge; a 7 kV/m travelling wave (34 V on a 30 mm period) carries them round at 5 cm/s, a lap in 16.6 s.
  Grains closer than 0.65 mm along the guide buckle from a line into a zigzag.
- Four 0.5 V gaps on a 932 mm loop, harmonic 13, one chirp 58.5 → 112.6 MHz in 21 µs with 90° between gaps: a
  continuous 50 eV beam reaches 185 eV in 135 laps, 45 % captured, 1.6 eV rms spread. On a 50 Ω pickup coupling
  30 %, 1e5 to 1e7 circulating electrons give about -98 to -58 dBm (an estimate).
- Cyclotron resonance at 2.45 GHz needs 87.5 mT uniform to 1e-4: 17 V/m gives 48 eV in 10 µs, 10 kV/m gives
  3.4 keV. It needs a static field, so it sits outside the RF-first plan.
- Two faults: the knot-against-ring solenoid comparison was confounded by the single-wire winding's net current
  (resolved by experiment 1), and one figure panel is titled with a field at which every electron was lost.

**Experiment 1** (`experiments/guide_study.md`; registered at 6c058df, run once, recorded at 85a3ff6). A
magnetic guide wound on five closed curves, 142 shots, ten criteria fixed beforehand.
- A flat ring lost all 10 electrons within 0.03 lap of the predicted lap. A trefoil, a (1,3) helix on a torus
  and a lifted figure-eight all held electrons. The unknotted helix held four times the gyroradius the trefoil
  did on axis.
- First-order theory: held or lost right in 92 of 93, reach within 15 % in 54 of 55, closed orbit in 21 of 22;
  turn per lap off by more than 8° in 7 fits of 22 (up to 16.5°), so the registered test failed on that.
- Read afterwards (hypothesis GDE-H2): the shift shared by both directions is ρ × bending / 2, to 2 % on seven
  of eight pairs. The reversing part matches how far the coil's own field lines turn beyond the holonomy on the
  helix (5.8°) and figure-eight (0.3°); on the trefoils that check was not clean and their 6.7° and 1.1° are
  open. The closed orbit's second-order error is open.
- Stated in its protocol: one shot was tracked for two laps in a timing test before registration.

## 4. Theory worked out and not yet in code or ledger

Leads to derive properly, prove where they are mathematics, and test. Each is marked with its status.

1. **Scaling law of an RF guide** (derived on paper, to enter the ledger). For a quadrupole of characteristic
   distance r₀ driven at amplitude V and angular frequency ω, the Mathieu parameter is `q = 2eV/(mω²r₀²)` and the
   pseudo-potential depth is `D = qeV/8` times a geometry factor. Holding q fixed, `ω ∝ √V / r₀` and `D ∝ V`.
   For electrons at q = 0.3 and r₀ = 1 mm: 5.4 V at 0.4 GHz gives 0.2 eV; 203 V at 2.45 GHz gives 7.6 eV. The
   array's 0.4-6 GHz band is the band in which millimetre-scale electrodes confine electrons.
2. **Energy held round a bend** (estimate, checked within a factor two by the first run). An RF guide holds
   kinetic energy up to about `W = D / (2 κ_max r₀)`, so `W ≈ (q/16)·eV·(R/r₀)` for bend radius R. Energy held
   grows with volts times R/r₀, with no ceiling other than the voltage: 100 eV takes 53 V at 1.25 GHz with
   R/r₀ = 100, 1 keV takes 530 V near 4 GHz. This is the statement to prove in answer to "are RF fields enough
   at any energy".
3. **What a knot costs an RF guide** (follows from CUR-3). Any closed curve of length L has `κ_max ≥ 2π/L`, a
   knotted one `κ_max > 4π/L`. So a knotted RF guide holds less than half the energy of a ring of the same
   length and electrodes.
4. **A guide printed on the board: the null of two rails** (derived on paper by complex analysis; to prove in
   Lean and test). Two RF rails in a grounded plane, each from a/2 to a/2 + b either side of the centre line,
   have complex field `f(z) = 1/(z−c₂) − 1/(z−c₁) + 1/(z+c₁) − 1/(z+c₂)`, `c₁ = a/2`, `c₂ = a/2 + b`. Its zero
   is at `z² = −c₁c₂`: the field null sits at height `h = √(c₁c₂) = ½√(a(a+2b))` above the board, the geometric
   mean of the rails' edge distances. The gradient there, `f′(ih)`, gives the secular frequency and the depth.
5. **The field of any electrode pattern from its outline** (a lead to verify before use). For an electrode at
   potential V in a grounded plane, the potential at a point is V/2π times the solid angle the electrode
   subtends, so its field is a line integral round the electrode's outline of the Biot-Savart form,
   `E = (V/2π)∮ dl × (x − x′)/|x − x′|³`. If the calibration confirms it, `Coil.field`'s kernel computes the RF
   field of a PCB pattern from its outline.
6. **Acceleration along the path.** Segments of the pattern driven with a phase step per segment from the
   array's coherent channels make a travelling wave; a chirp keeps it in step with the particle. The first
   run's four-gap model is the longitudinal map; energy per lap is `e·V_gap·n_gaps·sin φ_s`.
7. **The particles' own fields.** A charge moving past an electrode induces a current `i = q v·E_w` (the
   Shockley-Ramo theorem, with E_w the electrode's field per volt): the beam's own signal on the same
   electrodes, at harmonics of the revolution frequency. Space charge limits a pseudo-potential guide to a
   density `n = 2ε₀mω_sec²/e²`, about 1e15 m⁻³ at 212 MHz. Particles in a line repel into a chain and buckle
   into a zigzag below a spacing set by the guide's stiffness.
8. **Mathematics to use.**
   - Complex analysis: 2-D fields are analytic functions, multipoles are powers of x + iy, nulls are zeros, an
     array's pattern is a polynomial on the unit circle (N channels give at most N − 1 nulls: a root count,
     provable in Lean in a few lines).
   - Geometry of curves: writhe, holonomy, Fenchel, Fáry-Milnor (in use).
   - Algebraic geometry: a (p, q) torus knot is where `uᵖ = v^q` meets a 3-sphere; knotted null lines of a
     field can be built from such polynomials. Recalled from the optics literature, to verify. It would replace
     the optimiser's search for knotted fields in `experiments/local_fields.py` with a closed-form target.
