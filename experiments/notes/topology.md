# Antenna count, radio astronomy, software against physical limits: research notes (unreviewed)

## investigate:count

The count study is complete: for this array the knee is at 8 elements, a second smaller one at 12-16, and beyond 16 extra elements buy almost nothing while four radio channels are kept. All figures come from an idealised model (isotropic elements, no coupling or multipath, independent 3 deg calibration errors), so they are upper bounds on what hardware will do.

`count_study.py` and its main results already existed from an earlier run (13:49 today). I checked them, added an `--extras` mode (theory checks, equal-time experiment, fix time, astronomy, cost) and ran it. scipy is not installed in `.env`, so the extras use `math.erfc`.

**Theory, each checked by simulation**
- **Azimuth accuracy:** for a ring, sigma_az = s / (k r cos(el) sqrt(N/2)), with s^2 = sigma_cal^2 + 1/(2K rho). Closed form against Monte Carlo at 408 MHz: 1.64 vs 1.63 deg (N=8), 1.16 vs 1.11 (N=16), 0.58 vs 0.58 (N=64). Fitted exponents are -0.47 to -0.52 against the predicted -0.5.
- **Elevation:** a planar array has d(phase)/d(el) proportional to sin(el), so the bound diverges at the horizon. Planar 95th-percentile elevation error stays at 4-8 deg even at N=64. Rings at heights of +/-150 mm give 1.2 deg at N=16, matching the closed form (1.16 vs 1.21).
- **Shared channels:** at equal observing time, noise-limited accuracy is set by channels times time, not N. Simulated at -10 dB with 4 shared channels: 5.0 / 5.8 / 5.4 / 5.8 deg for N = 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 (flat). With N real channels it falls from 3.4 to 0.82 deg. When calibration-limited, shared equals full (1.11 vs 1.10 deg at N=16).
- **Sidelobes:** irregular layouts follow 10 log(ln M / N): -5.0 predicted, -4.7 measured at N=16, 2.3 GHz; -11.0 vs -10.5 at N=64. A single ring is stuck at -7.9 dB (J0), and two rings stacked 300 mm apart keep a -2.2 dB lobe at 2.3 GHz.
- **Gain:** follows 10 log N until N reaches the aperture's mode count, then saturates at 6.4 dBi (408 MHz, by N of 4-8), 10.2 (1 GHz, N about 12), 12.4 (1.42 GHz, N about 24-32) and 15.4 (2.3 GHz, N about 48).
- **Simultaneous sources (MUSIC, N channels):** min(N-1, about 2kr+3), giving 5-7 at 0.4 GHz, 13 at 1 GHz (N=16), 19 at 1.42 GHz (N=24) and 27 at 2.3 GHz (N=48).

**Fewest elements per job**

### Open questions

- Is the 3 deg calibration residual independent per element on real hardware? If part of it is common (multipath, manifold error), accuracy stops improving with N earlier than 1/sqrt(N) predicts.
- The ring of 8 passes with only a 0.6 dB sidelobe margin at 2.3 GHz in an isotropic, coupling-free model. Does it survive real whip patterns and coupling, or is 12-16 the true minimum for the 2-D estimator?
- The 3-D search budget was small and mostly returned the canonical stacked rings. A larger search (and the parallel array3d study, not yet written when I ran) may find better 6-12 element volumes.
- MUSIC source counts were tested for horizon sources on planar layouts only. Does a 3-D array resolve more when sources differ in elevation?
- Going beyond 16 external ports needs wider group selectors and more control bits than rev B has; that hardware cost and insertion loss are not in the $19 per element figure.
- The RA.314 band list came from a garbled extraction of the superseded 2003 edition; confirm against RA.314-11 before quoting band edges.

**What software and topology buy, and at what price**

- **More sources than N-1 (difference co-array).** A nested 8+8 line resolves 64 uncorrelated sources with 16 sensors (limit 71; a Wichmann ruler reaches 90). It needs at least 300 snapshots, does not improve with SNR, and fails completely when sources are coherent (multipath). For 8 sources it is twice as noisy as a plain uniform line.
- **On the 450 mm plate the limit is aperture, not N.** Azimuth degrees of freedom follow 2kD+1: about 13 at 408 MHz, 24 at 915 MHz, 32 at 1420 MHz, 48 at 2.3 GHz. Co-array methods beat 15 sources only above roughly 1 GHz.
- **Snapshot switching (rev B).** Four channels tied to four fixed port groups can measure 96 of the 120 pairs in 16 slots; the 24 same-group pairs are never measurable. With 16 slots a covariance fit stays within reach of 16 simultaneous radios up to about 6-8 sources (2.3 GHz, 8 sources: 92 % against 99 %). The 4-slot schedule (24 pairs) collapses beyond 4 sources. Bursty emitters cost the 16-slot scheme 5-20 points at 2-4 sources.
- **Frequency diversity.** A 4-whip ring at 0.63 wavelengths (1420 MHz) goes from 7.8 % gross errors to 0.4 % only when the emitter spans 10 % bandwidth, and to 0 % at 20 %. The 1400-1427 MHz band (1.9 %) and a 20-56 MHz capture do nothing. It helps only for hoppers or the board's own stepped signals.
- **Chinese-remainder baseline pairs.** P(correct) = erf(π / (σ√(2(p²+q²)))) matched Monte Carlo. At the 4.2° pair-phase error implied by a 3° residual, baselines of 8 and 9 half-wavelengths unwrap 99.95 % of the time; 15 and 16 give 94.6 %.
- **MIMO radar.** Four transmit antennas in a row at 100 mm beside the panel make a 16×4 virtual array at 5.8 GHz: 6.6° beam against 27.2° today, sidelobes -13.1 dB. The four existing quadrants transmitting orthogonal codes give 19.6° and -22.6 dB with no new hardware. Time-division transmit at 1 ms slots is unambiguous only to 3.2 m/s.
- **Rotation synthesis.** A 45° turntable sweep cuts the two-ring layout's worst sidelobe at 2.3 GHz from 0.24 to 0.067 without narrowing the beam. Six hours of Earth rotation on a 10 m field at 1420 MHz fill 52 % of uv cells (0.9 % static) and halve the worst sidelobe (0.27 to 0.12) at 0.9° resolution.
- **Super-resolution threshold.** At 915 MHz (38.6° beam), MUSIC splits two sources 0.25 beamwidth apart at 10 dB with a 1° residual (99 %), but only 78 % with 3°. At 0.1 beamwidth a 3° residual caps success near 25 % at any SNR. Calibration, not SNR, sets the floor.
- **Astronomy sensitivity (estimates).** Sixteen whips correlated at once reach 10σ on Cygnus A in 0.4 s at 408 MHz and 122 s at 1420 MHz. Switching 4 of 16 costs exactly 20× in time (41 min at 1420 MHz). The panel sees only the Sun. The plate resolves 27° at 1420 MHz, so real astronomy needs the whips on cables across 3-30 m and channelisation to about 2.3 MHz at 10 m.

**Judgement for array16.** Worth adopting: the 16-slot orthogonal schedule with covariance fitting, quadrant-MIMO radar codes, coprime baseline pairs for external antennas, and turntable synthesis for manifold work. Not worth building around: co-array source counts below 1 GHz, frequency diversity for narrowband emitters, learned DF without a measured manifold.

### Findings

- **Limit: at most N-1 sources. Difference co-array, mathematics** (high): For uncorrelated sources R = A P A^H + s2 I, so vec(R) = (A* kr A) p + s2 vec(I): the powers p see a virtual array at the pairwise differences x_i - x_j. SENSITIVITY:
- System temperature, whips at the board jack (estimate): 239 K at 408 MHz and 202 K at 1420 MHz. This is receiver 84-94 K + ground pickup 73-92 K + loss 29-44 K + sky 6-20 K, plus 0-100 K of man-made noise at 408 MHz. An LNA mounted at the whip lowers it to 151-198 K.

REACHABLE:
- **The Sun, in every band.** The 30-day medians are 28, 73, 115 and 152 sfu at 410, 1415, 2695 and 4995 MHz. Signal-to-noise per baseline per second is 40-60 on whips and 8-12 on panel quadrants.
- **The Milky Way H I line with a single whip.** The line area seen by a whip is 520-870 K km/s, from the N_HI map. That gives a peak of 7-17 K at the jack. Noise is 0.54 K per 1 km/s channel in 60 s with frequency switching, so signal-to-noise is 12-31 per minute. A 27 degree array beam pointed at the Galactic plane sees 38-58 K.

Cas A and Cyg A at 1420 MHz would rise above the noise in about 8 minutes with 4 channels, or 25 s with a hypothetical 16-channel correlator. The real limit is confusion. On plate baselines the diffuse Galaxy's visibilities are 80-200 times Cas A's (0.33-0.84 K against 4 mK), and still 2-3 times larger on 10-20 m baselines. Detecting them needs directive elements (dishes or horns) or full imaging; adding elements does not help.

NOT REACHABLE:
- Pulsars. B0329+54 takes about 110-120 h for 5 sigma with whips on the plate. It drops to about 3.5 h only with 16 whips spread at least half a wavelength apart, summed coherently, with an LNA at each whip.
- OH, CH and H2CO lines.
- Cas A with the panel, which needs 16-140 h.

ONE 16-ELEMENT BEAM VERSUS ALL BASELINES. For a point source the noise is nearly the same: 1/16 for the beam against 1/sqrt(240) for the correlator. Total power needs gain stability of 1e-3 (Sun) down to 1e-6 (Cas A), while correlations are immune to gain drift. Snapshot switching keeps only 6 simultaneous pairs, so noise is SEFD/sqrt(12Bt) whatever the number of whips. That is 4.6 times worse in flux, or 21 times longer, than 16 at once.

VARYING THE NUMBER OF ANTENNAS. With 4 channels, more whips add baselines (96 of 120 in 16 slots), not sensitivity, and fewer whips lose nothing. Sensitivity grows only with simultaneous channels (8 radios and 1.28 GB/s for 16) or with analog tiles of N/4. Analog tiles are the MWA/LOFAR approach, and rev B's panel quadrants already work this way.

Antenna orientation matters more than 3-D layout for astronomy:
- Vertical whips have zero gain at the zenith and 0.25 at 60 degrees up.
- The vertical panel covers 19% of the sky above 10 degrees within 3 dB.
- A panel facing the zenith covers 50%.

After 2000 s a signal of -184 dBm per 1 km/s channel at the whip port is detectable. Rev B's stray-calibration specification is -125 dBm, 59 dB too loud, so the LMX2572 must be powered down during observing.

Useful techniques:
- Correlations instead of total power.
- Walsh switching using the 0/180 polarity switches.
- Dicke switching against the on-board load, and frequency switching.
- Sidereal stacking, and turntable or Earth-rotation synthesis.
- Subtracting a model of the diffuse sky.
- Redundant calibration, and spectral-kurtosis RFI removal.

The Sun can also calibrate the direction-finding manifold in the restricted bands where rev B may not radiate. On plate baselines the unmodelled phase error from the Galaxy is 3-9 degrees at 1420 MHz, under 1 degree on baselines of 2 m or more, and 36-54 degrees at 408 MHz.
