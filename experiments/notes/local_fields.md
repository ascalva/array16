# Sculpting local 3-D fields (spots, vortex lines, knots): research notes (unreviewed)

## investigate:theory

Theory answer to "can fields twist into each other to make exotic local shapes": yes as interference patterns, no as objects. Fields in vacuum do not act on each other, so everything the array makes is E(r) = sum of w_n G_n(r): 16 fixed patterns and 16 complex numbers. Checks used scalar point sources (no coupling, no element pattern), so numbers are estimates.

**1. Degrees of freedom.** Usable shapes = min(N, about pi*A/lambda^2), and each imposed zero or spot costs one, so at most N-1 point constraints.
- Panel (100 mm aperture): pi*A/lambda^2 is 2.1 at 2.44 GHz and 11.8 at 5.8 GHz; SVD over a 0.3 m box gives 6 and 16 modes within 20 dB.
- Whip plate (450 mm): 0.9 / 4.7 / 11.2 / 29.4 at 0.4 / 0.915 / 1.42 / 2.3 GHz; SVD gives 6 / 12 / 16 / 16.
- Rev B transmit is much poorer: four channel weights plus quantised delays on the panel, and four whips (X0-X3). Independent per-element control would add amplitude tapers, exact zero placement, superoscillation and 16-waveform time reversal. On receive all 16 weights exist in software.

**2. Focusing and quiet zones.** Spot width is about 0.886*lambda*z/D with a floor near lambda/2; depth of focus about 7.1*lambda*(z/D)^2. A true 3-D focus needs Fresnel number D^2/(4*lambda*z) above 1, i.e. z below 20 mm / 48 mm (panel 2.44 / 5.8 GHz) and 68 / 155 / 388 mm (plate 0.4 / 0.915 / 2.3 GHz).
- The panel therefore only narrows a beam: computed FWHM at z = 100 mm is 116 mm (2.44 GHz) and 52 mm (5.8 GHz), with focusing gain of 0.2 and 1.1 dB.
- The plate at 2.3 GHz and z = 200 mm gives a 79 mm spot and +13.7 dB over unfocused weights.
- A quiet ball at -20 dB needs 1 / 4 / 9 / 16 constraints for radius 0.05 / 0.20 / 0.35 / 0.50 lambda. With an error-aware design and rev B's 3 deg / 0.26 dB residual, a 0.2-lambda ball sits 31-35 dB below the bright point, at a bright-point cost of 0.9-4.8 dB.
- Sub-wavelength detail of period L decays 54.6 dB per L of distance.

**3. Singularities.** Zeros of a complex scalar field are lines, with integer charge; only charge 1 is stable. Adding a uniform term to an l=1 excitation slides the vortex sideways.
- Both arrays can make a dark ring threaded by an axial vortex line: two concentric l=1 rings and one complex ratio. Verified with winding +1 at 16 of 16 azimuths and on the axis in seven cases.
- These are zeros of one field component, not of total intensity.
- C-points need dual-polarised elements (32 ports); they are vortices of one circular component.
- Closed Hopf links and trefoils are out of reach. The optical trefoil used five Laguerre-Gaussian modes, but synthesising them needs a wide, finely sampled aperture: an acoustic replica needed at least 12x12 pixels for the link and 16x16 for the trefoil on an 8-lambda aperture. The arrays have 16 elements on 0.6-3.5 lambda. My own linear link test was inconclusive, even for 256-element reference arrays.

**4. Time reversal.** Reflections act as image sources, so the spot tends to lambda/2 regardless of aperture. Peak over background is about N_antennas x B/B_c: for four channels, 20-56 MHz and 25-50 ns delay spread, 11-19 dB (estimate). It needs a probe or beacon at the target.

**5. Superoscillation.** Halving the spot costs 12.5 dB of spot power with sidelobes 1.8 dB above it; a quarter-size spot costs 25.5 dB with sidelobes +15 dB and an error floor only 11 dB down. A factor of about 0.6 is usable.

**6. Impossible.**
- Field-field interaction: vacuum nonlinearity scales as (E/1.3e18 V/m)^2, about 1e-38 at 10 V/m.
- Self-confined objects: for free fields the mean-square radius of the energy grows as c^2*t^2; a focus is energy passing through.
- Force or heat: FCC 15.249 (50 mV/m at 3 m, -1.25 dBm EIRP) gives about 0.75 V/m at 0.2 m, some 6,700 times below the 1 mW/cm^2 exposure limit.

**Useful:** receive-side near-field focusing and nulling, steerable null lines as position markers, and time-reversal experiments.

### Findings

- **Linearity / what 'twist into each other' can mean** (high): In vacuum the array field is E(r)=sum_n w_n G_n(r): 16 fixed element patterns times 16 complex weights. Fields superpose and do not act on each other; every 'exotic shape' is an interference pattern. Vacuum nonlinearity (Euler-Heisenberg) scales as (E/E_cr)^2 with E_cr = m^2c^3/(e*hbar) = 1.32e18 V/m; at 10 V/m the fractional effect is of order 1e-38 (estimate, prefactor alpha/45pi ~ 5e-5 times (7.6e-18)^2). Photon-photon scattering has only been seen in Pb-Pb collisions (ATLAS, 8.2 sigma). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwinger_limit ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03536 ; derivation from linear Maxwell equations
- **1. Degrees of freedom: formula** (high): Number of independent field features (spots, nulls, singularity positions) an aperture can impose = min(N elements, number of propagating modes ~ pi*A/lambda^2 for a planar aperture into a half space; paraxially A_aperture*A_target/(lambda*z)^2 between two facing areas). K point constraints (zeros or prescribed values) cost K of N complex weights, so at most N-1 exact zeros with one weight left for overall level. Depth adds no modes unless the region is close compared with its depth (Miller). Source: https://www.doi.org/10.1364/AO.39.001681 (Miller 2000, abstract) ; https://doi.org/10.1109/8.29386 (Bucci and Franceschetti 1989, DOI from memory, not re-fetched) ; derivation: plane-wave count A*pi*k^2/(2pi)^2

Sub-wavelength near-field detail** (high): A pattern of spatial period L < lambda is evanescent with amplitude decay exp(-z*sqrt((2pi/L)^2 - k^2)), about 54.6 dB per L of distance when L << lambda. - **3. Phase singularities** (high): A complex scalar field vanishes where Re = Im = 0: two conditions, so zeros are lines in 3-D (vortex lines), around which phase winds by 2*pi*s with integer charge s; only s = +-1 is stable (higher charges split under any error), lines end nowhere (closed loops or infinite), and they are created or destroyed in pairs or as loops. Near a line psi ~ a*(x + i*y), so adding a uniform component c moves the zero by -c/a: two real numbers steer the line transversely; pinning a line through K points costs K constraints, spaced no wider than the resolution cell. Source: https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1974.0012 (Nye and Berry 1974, DOI from memory, not re-fetched) ; derivation
- **3. A linked structure the arrays can make: threaded vortex ring** (medium): Driving two concentric groups with one phase turn (l = 1) gives psi = f(rho,z)*exp(i*phi): an axial vortex line plus a dark ring wherever complex f = 0, and one complex ratio a between the groups places that ring at a chosen (rho0, z0). Verified: winding +1 around the ring core at 16 of 16 azimuths and +1 around the axis at three heights in all seven cases. Plate 8+8 whips: 2.3 GHz ring radius 60 mm at z = 150 mm (inner/outer ratio -3.8 dB) and 100 mm at 250 mm (-7.7 dB; core wanders 1 mm in radius, 2.5 mm in height); 0.915 GHz 120 mm at 200 mm; 0.4 GHz 150 mm at 200 mm. Needs per-element amplitude on two groups: not available on rev B transmit (whips transmit on 4 ports; panel quadrants do not match inner/outer), available on receive. - **3. Vector caveat** (high): A vortex is a zero of one field component. All three components of E vanishing at once is six conditions and does not generically occur in 3-D, so a 'dark line' is dark for a probe of one polarisation; for an l = 1 ring of vertical whips the transverse field components do not vanish on the axis. In the panel's paraxial region E_y dominates and the total is nearly dark. Source: derivation (codimension count)
- **3. Polarisation singularities** (medium): C-lines (circular polarisation) are where E.E = 0, one complex condition, so lines in 3-D; in a transverse plane they appear as C-points with index +-1/2 (lemon, star, monstar) separated by L-lines of linear polarisation. A C-point is a vortex of one circular component (zero of E_x - i*E_y or E_x + i*E_y), so with dual-polarised elements (32 ports) it is placed and steered exactly like a scalar vortex. The single-polarised panel has fixed polarisation in its paraxial field and only weak, hardly controllable C-lines from near-field longitudinal components. Knotted polarisation singularities have been reconstructed in optics. Source: https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1983.0109 (Nye 1983) ; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-018-0229-2 (Larocque et al. 2018) (both DOIs from memory, not re-fetched) ; derivation
- **3. Knotted and linked vortex lines in optics** (high): Leach, Dennis, Courtial and Padgett (Nature 432, 165, 2004) made linked and knotted dark threads by superposing four beams on a spatial light modulator: three with the same axial vortex strength and different numbers of radial nodes, plus a Gaussian. Dennis, King, Jack, O'Holleran and Padgett (Nature Physics 6, 118, 2010) made isolated Hopf links, trefoils and cinquefoils from Laguerre-Gaussian superpositions built from braided-zero polynomials and numerically optimised for contrast; the trefoil is five modes, (radial, azimuthal) = (0,0), (1,0), (2,0), (3,0), (0,3). Random wave fields contain vortex loops, occasionally threaded or linked; about 73 % of lines percolate. - **3. Can 16 or 32 RF elements make a Hopf link or trefoil** (medium): Almost certainly not as closed isolated loops. Few modes are needed (4-5) but they are wide paraxial beams with up to three radial nodes (mode order 2p+|l| = 6, beam quality factor 7), which demand an aperture of roughly 6-8 lambda sampled finely. The one wave experiment with a discrete aperture (acoustic, 6 kHz, lambda/3 cells, 24x24 on about 8 lambda) found minimums of 12x12 pixels for the Hopf link and 16x16 for the trefoil, with knots about 3.5-4.4 lambda across and 7 lambda long. The arrays have 16 elements on 0.8-1.9 lambda (panel) or 0.6-3.5 lambda (plate): about ten times too few elements and 2-10 times too little aperture; 32 elements do not change that. A loop linked with an infinite axial line (previous finding) is the achievable substitute. - **6. No self-confined or self-sustaining field objects** (high): For source-free Maxwell fields energy-momentum conservation and the traceless stress tensor give d^2/dt^2 of the integral of r^2*u = 2*(total energy), so the mean-square radius of any finite-energy field grows as c^2*t^2: nothing stays put without sources or matter. For a steady single-frequency field the net time-averaged power through any closed surface containing no source is zero, so a focus or vortex is energy passing through (the energy in a 50 mm spot is replaced in about 0.2 ns), and it disappears when the transmitter stops. ### Open questions

- Hopf link / trefoil feasibility is argued from mode order, aperture and the acoustic experiment's pixel minimums, not proven: my linear 'dark curve' test failed even for 256-element reference arrays, so it is inconclusive. A proper test needs the braided-polynomial (Milnor) construction projected onto the array's 16 patterns plus 3-D line tracing; the sibling script's 'curves' section may settle it.

**Short answer to the owner.** Fields do not twist "into each other": Maxwell's equations in vacuum are linear, so the field is the sum of 16 fixed element patterns and the only controls are 16 complex numbers. What is real is interference structure: bright spots, nulls, and dark vortex lines the phase winds around. These can be placed and steered locally, but they are patterns in a wave that keeps radiating outward, not self-contained objects.

**Model.** Exact Hertzian-dipole fields with all near-field terms, ground images, no mutual coupling, 3 deg / 0.26 dB element errors. It is not a full-wave result.

**What works with rev B hardware**
- **Steerable vortex line:** a charge-1 dark line along the beam axis, steered to 15 and 30 deg, lands within about 1 deg at z = 0.2-0.4 m, at 2.4 and 5.8 GHz. Rev B's quantised delays move it by 2.5 mm or less. Charge 2 splits into two charge-1 lines 22 mm apart at 5.8 GHz.
