# AGENTS.md: orientation for an agent working here

Read this, then `docs/PROGRESS.md` (where the work stands, what is next, and how to pick it up).

## What the owner is after

A phased-array software radio, and a research question about its fields. The aim, in the owner's words
(2026-11-20): **"the point of all this theory and experiments is can you build a generic process for creating
arbitrary fields that can allow this to act like an antenna, a virtual antenna? studying that and understanding the
theory aspect and consequences of such as system"** (`docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md`).

The particle work is one case of it: **can the field generated by the array produce a closed geometric shape that
holds charged particles and accelerates them along the path, and what do different geometries and configurations
yield when it comes to the fields produced by the particles themselves?** It may sound like science fiction. The job
is to find out what is physically and experimentally plausible: to investigate and prove out ideas, even where they
stay theoretical.

## The owner's rules

These were given as corrections, so they bind.

1. **Radio-frequency fields first, and everything on the array's boards.** The plan is the array's own fields. Coils
   and electrodes are welcome only where they are part of the PCB design, integrated into the array itself: traces,
   planar windings, electrode patterns on its boards. No external or additional hardware that has to be set up
   beside the array. The array may span several boards arranged in three dimensions (stacked, tiled, at angles,
   flexible): one board would hold a guide to a planar ring, and a knot takes a path out of the plane (owner,
   2026-11-21). Keep the options open while the work is early: a ring with everything on one board and knots across
   several boards are designs to weigh, not lines committed to (owner, 2026-11-22: "keep options open, be flexible
   while in the early stages"). An external coil or magnet needs it first fully shown that RF fields alone do not
   suffice at any energy, and then counts only as a budget-friendly option. Experiment 1 (a magnetic guide wound on a
   former) was a detour from this and is finished; do not extend it unasked.
2. **Never dismiss the question.** Write what a thing takes and how to get there ("1 eV takes 12 kV/m at
   0.4 GHz; elements a millimetre from the path reach that with tens of volts"), not that something "does
   nothing".
3. **Write what is there, or what will be there.** No "nothing is built", no lists of what is missing, no
   safety sections. A number that bears on a design choice goes where the choice is made.
4. **Theory carries weight.** Derive first, then predict, then test. Prove the mathematics in Lean (`formal/`,
   `docs/LAWS.md`). Reach for stronger machinery where it helps: complex analysis, the geometry of curves,
   algebraic geometry.
5. **Run it as physics.** Calibrate an instrument before using it. Register predictions and criteria before a
   run (`array16.lab` enforces this). Keep a case that must fail. Label claims (Proposition, Claim,
   Hypothesis). Keep what is read from results afterwards apart, as a hypothesis.
6. **Code.** `uv` for everything; `ruff` and `mypy --strict`; tests with pytest and Hypothesis; immutable
   objects and pure functions where they fit. The core must be robust, reliable, flexible and fast enough to be
   reused for any experiment, simulation, topology, design or resource setup. Resources are compute and
   hardware alike: a HackRF Pro, antennas, PCBs, microcontrollers, motors, the network (`benches/`).
7. **Open source only.** The tools used, and the tools made here. The repository is public, AGPL-3.0.
8. **Hardware must be buildable at home.** Cheap, reliable parts for an amateur setup in a Brooklyn apartment.
   No industrial equipment. The radio is the HackRF Pro alone, one coherent channel (decided 2026-11-20), and it
   alone runs from USB power; the board and whatever drives its electrodes may have their own supply, sized to the
   design.
9. **Show it.** Graphs, diagrams and 3-D figures that carry the experiments, the theory and the intuition.
10. **Spend tokens on purpose.** Few sub-agents; no speculative fan-out.
11. **No absolute local paths** in any tracked file.
12. **Simulate first, build last.** The work is theory, designs and simulation experiments. Custom boards are
    designed for fabrication and built once those are understood well enough to finalise the design (owner,
    2026-11-21). Until then experiments run on simulated benches, and hardware choices wait for that stage.

## How work lands

- Commits: `type(scope): subject` (`feat fix docs test refactor perf ops chore ci`), imperative, at most 72
  characters, one logical change, the body the why. Stage every path by name. Pass the message with
  `git commit -F -` and a quoted heredoc. `Co-Authored-By` on agent-written work. Never amend or rebase what is
  pushed. Push to `origin main`; CI runs both gates.
- Gates before every commit: `uv run ruff format --check`, `uv run ruff check`, `uv run mypy`, `uv run pytest -q`,
  and `./formal/check.sh` when `formal/` or `docs/LAWS.md` changed.
- New Python joins the lists in `pyproject.toml` (`[tool.ruff] include`, `[tool.mypy] files`).
- A new law gets a row in `docs/LAWS.md`; `tests/test_laws.py` holds the ledger, the code, the tests and the
  Lean declarations to each other.
- A new experiment is an `array16.lab.Experiment` with a `PROTOCOL.md`; `run` starts once its predictions are
  committed. `experiments/guide_study.py` is the worked example.

## Where things are

- `array16/particles/`: the particle core. `array16/lab.py`: the harness. `benches/`: resource setups.
- `experiments/`: studies and experiments, each a script, its outputs and a report.
- `docs/VIRTUAL_ANTENNA.md`: the theory of the virtual antenna and of the generic process that makes it (the field
  compiler), its proposed laws and its experiments.
- `docs/LAWS.md`, `formal/`: the ledger and its proofs. `docs/PROGRESS.md`: the state and the plan.
- `README.md` for a reader; `ARCHITECTURE.md` and `REDESIGN.md` for the radio.
